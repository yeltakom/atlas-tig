The flooding of a valley

The Ilısu Dam began impounding water on 19 July 2019. This platform gathers what happened in the Tigris valley into dossiers. Each dossier asks a question, builds the map around that question, and shows the finding, the method, the source and the question that is still open side by side.

Working version. The results on the map are based on calculation and have not been verified on site. Each record states its source.

              Water surface within the lake boundary, by year

              A rough estimate. The share of dark surface within the lake boundary was measured in annual Sentinel-2 mosaics. The 8 to 9 per cent before filling is the riverbed and shadows. An annual mosaic does not show a single day, and an exact measurement needs dated imagery and an infrared band.

              Method and sources

              Method

              The lake boundary polygon was intersected with the settlement points and map layers. For each settlement, we calculated whether it lies inside the boundary and, if not, how many metres away from it. For roads and waterways, we calculated the length inside the boundary, and for areas the share inside.

              Limitations

              • Settlement coordinates were compiled from old topographic maps, and some may be off by up to 1 km.
              • The same village may appear twice under two different names. Hamlets and abandoned places are also counted.
              • Alternative names are compiled data. They carry no information on language or origin.
              • The source of the lake boundary layer is uncertain. Its calculated area is 318 km², and the official figure is 313 km².
              • The road and land layers rely on volunteer mapping and are incomplete.
              • Kurdish, Armenian and Arabic names and village populations are taken from Wikidata records and have not been verified against a primary source.
              • The location of some archaeological sites is approximate, and the site card says so.
              • Daily MODIS imagery has a resolution of 250 metres, and the ground is not visible on cloudy days.
              • Monument locations are taken from volunteer mapping and have not been verified on site.
              • Event dates are compiled from published sources. Where the sources disagree, the record carries a note.
              • Interviews and the researcher's field locations will not be published until the consent process is complete.
              • For high-resolution archive images, the date is the publication date, not the date the image was taken.
